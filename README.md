# Project Title

The Influence of Obesity and Body Shape on Sagittal Plane Knee Kinematics and Kinetics during Obstacle Crossing

## Description
Altered walking mechanics in individuals with obesity can contribute to knee osteoarthritis (KOA). These gait deviations may become more pronounced during obstacle crossing. In women, body fat distribution may further influence KOA risk, especially when excess fat accumulates in the thighs and hips. However, relatively little is known about how regional fat accumulation affects gait in women with obesity. This study investigated the influence of obesity and different fat distributions in women (Apple: more abdominal fat; Pear: more lower-limb fat) on knee biomechanics during walking with and without obstacle crossing. Participants were 15 controls without obesity (NB) and 27 with obesity (OB). Within female participants, 10 without obesity (fNB) were compared with 20 with obesity, stratified by waist-hip ratio (Apple:10, Pear:10). Speed-adjusted statistical parametric mapping applied a general linear model (NB vs OB) and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA, fNB vs Apple vs Pear). OB exhibited a significantly greater knee extension moment compared to NB during late stance across all tasks, and this difference persisted among fNB, Apple, and Pear (all, p<0.05). OB walked with reduced knee flexion during the early-stance leading limb after crossing a medium-height obstacle (OCMA-LL, p=0.048) and high-height obstacle (OCHA-LL, p=0.008) compared to NB. The ANCOVA revealed significant body-shape effects (p<0.05) and the post-hoc pairwise comparisons confirmed that Pear had decreased knee angles compared to fNB in OCMA-LL (p=0.008) and OCHA-LL (p=0.001). These findings suggest that obstacle crossing helps illuminate how excess weight influences knee biomechanics, and how regional fat distribution modulates the degree of this alteration.
